# Morty — case-study voice profile

- Original BetaTrends Notion case study
- Direct ownership confirmation captured on 7 September 2026

## Observable writing tendencies

- Direct and practical: explains what happened without a long setup.
- Uses first person naturally when describing personal work: “I recommended,” “I monitored,” “I created.”
- Often connects an action to a simple reason: “to better understand,” “to move faster,” or “before handing it off.”
- Prefers familiar words such as “problem,” “change,” “idea,” “design,” “test,” and “improve” over abstract design language.
- Sounds most natural when describing a real decision or activity, not when summarising the project in corporate language.
- Uses short conclusion-led headings and occasional questions to help unfamiliar readers.
- Is candid about what was difficult and what still needed work.

## Voice rules for this case study

- Use simple, spoken-professional sentences.
- Keep confirmed first-person ownership visible.
- Prefer “I reviewed the recordings and made a list of problems” to “behavioural evidence informed prioritisation.”
- Explain product terms once in plain language.
- Keep evidence limitations clear, but state them plainly and briefly.
- Do not imitate the original grammar mistakes or add artificial slang.
- Avoid portfolio clichés, inflated confidence, and overly polished summary phrases.
