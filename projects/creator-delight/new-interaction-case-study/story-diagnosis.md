# Story diagnosis

## Audience and intended decision

Primary assumed reader: a Product Design or UX Design hiring manager assessing product thinking, interaction design, evidence use, collaboration, and delivery. The user did not provide a specific target company or seniority, so the draft avoids role-specific positioning beyond those demonstrated capabilities.

## Context

BetaTrends was a real-time market-research platform. Businesses and creators used a multi-stage Interaction flow to prepare content, questions, screening rules, audience demographics, preview, pricing, and publication.

## Struggle

The recovered journey map and historical case study describe a long form, repeated modals, unclear cost feedback, weak preview discoverability, late incomplete-step messages, hard-to-manage questions, and competing buttons. Some evidence came from assumptions; Hotjar and Maze details are incompletely preserved.

## Transformation

The design evolved into a staged workspace with persistent orientation, preview and price context, distinct question/screening areas, explicit step states, and clearer action hierarchy. Morty confirmed his contribution through handoff. Production implementation and post-launch impact remain unverified.

## Turning point

Prototype testing revealed that the broad layout redesign had not resolved late validation, question management, or CTA confusion. This gave the story a credible reason for another iteration.

## Authorship boundary

Morty confirmed journey mapping, initial flow work, Hotjar review, three concepts, prototype creation, Maze testing, workshops, and handoff. Collaboration was primarily with Pieter and included workshop input from others. Final decision authority and wider participant roles remain unclear.

## Recommended structure

Outcome-first opening followed by Context–Struggle–Transformation. Use the Maze findings as the turning point. This replaces method headings with insight-led sections and keeps the strongest supported transformation visible.
