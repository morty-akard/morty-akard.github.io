The Product Owner requested the feature for an existing Game Room. The approved story describes an administrator needing to hand ownership to another participant before leaving. The historical narrative mentions manager review before testing; this does not establish a formal approval process or developer sign-off. Keep that detail optional rather than inflate collaboration.

The user story/HMW and checked task items are relevant process evidence. The old page's tags (such as competitor analysis) do not prove those methods were performed for this feature.

### Maze findings and definitions

The preserved PC task reads: “Please transfer ownership to player 2 Jesica with PC.”

- 24 participants in total.
- **0 direct successes; 20 indirect successes; 4 unfinished attempts.**
- The report defines indirect success as completing the mission through unexpected paths. Twenty participants did complete; this was not universal failure.
- Mission overview: 64.3% misclick rate and 32.6-second average duration. These are report metrics, not the percentage of people who failed or a before/after comparison.
- The indirect-path panel displays 54.0%; additional screen panels display 52.4% and 73.3%. These have different scopes from the mission's 64.3%; do not combine or substitute them.
- The path/heatmap and Morty's analysis support the initial-action discoverability interpretation. Heatmap colour alone cannot explain intent or prove all participants misunderstood right-click.

### Revision and supported outcome

The desktop visual shows an ellipsis trigger on a participant card and the transfer action in the open menu. The mobile/landscape visual shows the open menu; it does not clearly establish the closed-state trigger or a complete touch flow.

Morty's account says right-click was retained alongside the button. If used, describe this as intended design behaviour, not verified implementation; the rationale for retention is unknown.
